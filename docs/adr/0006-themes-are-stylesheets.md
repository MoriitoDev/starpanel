# Themes are stylesheets, and the contract is small

A Theme used to be fourteen colour Tokens plus a light/dark mode. It is now a CSS file the owner imports into `themes/`, layered over the baseline that ships in `app.css`, with the Dashboard document storing only a name. Two consequences are worth recording, because a future reader will otherwise propose the opposite. First, **an imported Theme may load remote resources** — `@import`, a webfont, a background image — and the panel will not stop it: the promise that Star Panel works offline covers what Star Panel ships, not what the owner installs. Second, **what a Theme author may rely on is deliberately small**: the token variables, the component classes in `app.css`, and six `data-part` attributes (`app`, `header`, `identity`, `card`, `empty`, `error`). Anything else a Theme does with our markup is allowed but unsupported, because a promise we cannot keep is worse than no promise.
